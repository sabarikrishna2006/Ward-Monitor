Motivation

Why change anything — NEWS2 alone isn't enough for DCM

The problem

NEWS2 is a fixed rule on current vitals. It was designed for acute deterioration (sepsis-style spikes). But dilated-cardiomyopathy decompensation is often a slow congestive drift — weight gain, fluid overload, rising filling pressures — that NEWS2 is structurally blind to. Result: poor accuracy and too many false alarms.

The two changes we made

  • Reframe the question. Not “is the score high now?” but “how long until this patient deteriorates?” — a survival / time-to-event model.
  • Add HF-specific signals on top of NEWS2: weight trend, fluid balance, NT-proBNP, lactate, kidney function, rhythm, comorbidity burden.

02The cohort

Who the model is for — CCU patients (with a DCM focus)

CCU only — and why

We trained on the Coronary Care Unit — the medical cardiology ICU — the CCU setting this model targets. MIMIC only charts vitals inside the ICU, so a ward model isn't yet possible from this data. We excluded CVICU (cardiac surgery ICU) — its escalations are post-operative, not medical heart-failure deterioration.

General vs DCM — we do both

The model is trained on all CCU cardiology patients for statistical power, but up-weights DCM patients 3×, carries a DCM indicator feature, and is evaluated separately on a held-out DCM subgroup. So it's a general CCU model that is explicitly tuned and checked for DCM.

03The rules — what counts as "deterioration"

The event we predict is more than NEWS2

The survival target is the time until the first deterioration event, defined as the earliest of two things — plus death handled carefully:

① Treatment escalation objective

The clinician acted: started a vasopressor / inotrope, put the patient on a ventilator, or started dialysis. This is independent of the vitals we feed in — so it's a real outcome, not circular.

② Sustained NEWS2 ≥ 7

The high-risk NEWS2 band, held over 2 consecutive hours. NEWS2 ≥ 7 is itself a clinical action threshold; the 2-hour rule filters out one-off charting blips.

Death is a competing risk — not folded in

A patient who dies without a preceding deterioration is censored, not counted as an event. Reason: a slow, preventable death is preceded by escalation or NEWS2 ≥ 7 (so we still catch it early), while a sudden unpredictable death has no vital-sign warning and shouldn't be forced onto a vitals model.

Why not just "NEWS2 crossed a line"?

Because predicting NEWS2 from NEWS2 would be circular and would inherit NEWS2's blind spot for DCM. Anchoring the event mostly on treatment escalation makes it a genuine outcome. Soft NEWS2 rises (4→5, 5→6) are kept as features, never as the event itself.

On the real cohort: 6,844 stays reach a deterioration event (4,167 by escalation, 5,424 by sustained NEWS2 ≥ 7), and 1,471 in-hospital deaths are handled as competing risks.

03bTurning stays into training examples

The sliding-window setup

  • Irregular ICU charting is snapped to a regular hourly grid per patient.
  • At every hour t we make one training example (an "anchor").
  • Features come from the 6-hour look-back window [t−6h, t].
  • The label is time until the next deterioration, capped at a 24-hour horizon.
  • If no event happens (discharge / death / horizon) the example is censored — the survival model still uses it.
  • We skip anchors already at NEWS2 ≥ 7 (already critical) or with < 2 readings.

04Feature engineering

Two clinical axes — 94 features

Every feature is computed only from data at or before t (a leakage guard asserts this). We deliberately built two axes: the acute vital-sign axis NEWS2 already knows, and a heart-failure "congestion & substrate" axis it doesn't.

Axis 1 · Acute (what NEWS2 sees)
  • Per vital (HR, RR, SpO₂, SBP, DBP, temp): last, mean, std, min, max, rate, Δ-from-admission
  • NEWS2 dynamics: current value, mean, max, rate of change, hours-in-band
  • Oxygen / FiO₂ / consciousness flags
Axis 2 · Congestion & substrate (the DCM layer)
  • Weight trend Δ24h / Δ72h + ESC (≥2 kg/3 d) & HFSA (≥0.9 kg/1 d) alert flags
  • Urine-output rate (fluid status)
  • Labs: NT-proBNP, troponin, lactate, sodium, eGFR, Hb, K, INR, platelets — with missingness flags
  • Cardiac rhythm (atrial fibrillation / VT-VF)
  • Age, sex, Charlson comorbidity index
Why rate-of-change matters

A heart rate rising 80 → 110 over 4 hours is more dangerous than a patient stable at 110 — even though NEWS2 scores them the same. Our *_rate features capture exactly that trajectory.

Data reality check: NT-proBNP (the key HF marker) is present for ~95% of examples; sparser labs carry an explicit "missing" flag because missingness is itself informative. Features MIMIC lacks — HRV, cardiac-MRI strain/scar, bio-impedance, structured NYHA class — are documented as future additions for when such data becomes available.

05The model

XGBoost AFT — a survival model that handles censoring

What "AFT" means, simply

AFT = Accelerated Failure Time. Ordinary regression can't learn from a patient who was simply discharged without deteriorating — you don't know when they would have. AFT can: it accepts “the event happened at hour T” for events and “the event hadn't happened by hour T” for censored patients, and learns from both. It fits a probability distribution over time-to-event, so it naturally gives a median and an uncertainty band.

Choices we made

  • Gradient-boosted trees (survival:aft), logistic distribution (chosen by validation score)
  • Handles missing values natively; captures non-linear interactions
  • Conformal calibration for the 5–95% band — because the raw model band was over-confident
  • Death as a competing risk; DCM rows up-weighted 3×

07Interpretability

What the model actually uses

Where the extra features help

On the general CCU population, the congestion features barely move the needle (full ≈ NEWS2-only). Their value is concentrated in DCM, where they lift C-index 0.785 → 0.804 and cut alerts 4.0 → 3.6. That's the clinically-honest finding: HF-specific features matter for the HF phenotype.

08Limitations

Scope and limitations

It's an ICU proof-of-concept

Trained on US ICU data (MIMIC-IV, 2008–2019), not the general ward. Needs prospective external validation and recalibration before clinical use.

DCM numbers are uncertain

Only 161 DCM test events — the 0.80 C-index has a wide interval (0.68–0.90). Real, but preliminary.

It's a risk ranking, not a countdown

Because most patients never deteriorate, the raw "expected time" is optimistic. We surface the calibrated band and a risk rank, not a literal "6 hours."

Some inputs are proxies

Consciousness is derived from GCS; rhythm is sparsely charted; NYHA class isn't in MIMIC at all — it will be captured via a labeling workflow in deployment for future fine-tuning.
