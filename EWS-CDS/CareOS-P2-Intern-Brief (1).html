Who you're building for

Primary users are bedside nurses and charge nurses — not cardiologists, not administrators. This is the single most important frame for every design and engineering decision you make.

Bedside Nurse — Primary Charge Nurse — Primary Attending / Resident — Secondary (alert recipient) Clinical Informatics — Evaluation

Nurses work at a station monitoring multiple patients simultaneously. The UI must support quick scan, immediate triage, minimal clicks. Every second of friction in a deterioration scenario is a product failure.

What you're building — two modules

Module A

Early Warning Score Engine & Deterioration Predictor

A real-time patient acuity dashboard ranked by deterioration risk. Each patient row shows:

  • Auto-calculated NEWS2 / MEWS score from latest charted vitals
  • 6-hour trend sparkline: HR, RR, SpO₂, BP, Temp, Consciousness
  • Colour-coded ML risk flag: green / amber / red (LSTM or XGBoost)
  • Model predicts deterioration 4–6 hours before the event using combinations of trending values — not single threshold breaches

When a patient crosses amber threshold → structured escalation prompt with patient name, bed, specific risk factors, and recommended nursing response.

Secondary panel: Sepsis-3 and KDIGO AKI criteria with time of first breach.

Module B

Drug–Lab Interaction Checker

A background process that continuously cross-references each patient's active medication list against their latest lab panel using a structured rule library (RxNorm + FDA).

Alerts appear as a badge on the patient row — one-line plain-English explanation + specific lab value that triggered it. Full rule logic shown on click.

Example rules:

TriggerAlert
K⁺ > 5.5 + ACE inhibitorHyperkalaemia risk — hold dose
eGFR < 30 + metformin activeLactic acidosis risk — review dose
Creatinine ↑ + NSAID activeAKI risk — prescriber review
INR > 3.5 + anticoagulantBleeding risk — check schedule

Why rule-based, not LLM: Clinical rules must be auditable and fully transparent. Drug-lab interactions follow deterministic logic — RxNorm + FDA sourcing ensures every alert can be traced to a published clinical guideline.

How success is measured

Module A — Early Warning
  • AUROC > 0.80 on MIMIC-IV held-out test set
  • Alert lead time: median hours between first amber and recorded clinical event
  • False positive rate at fixed sensitivity (nurse workload proxy)
  • NEWS2 score accuracy vs. manually computed ground truth
Module B — Drug-Lab
  • Rule coverage: % of known clinically significant interactions captured
  • Alert precision: % of alerts clinically valid on chart review
  • Alert timeliness: % generated before implicated dose administered
