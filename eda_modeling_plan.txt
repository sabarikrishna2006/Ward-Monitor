EDA & Modeling Plan: Dynamic Cost Variance Prediction

1. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Strategy
Before training the model, we need to understand what drives costs for Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) patients. Our EDA will focus on:
- Cost Distribution Analysis: Plot a histogram of the "Final Real Cost" across all DCM patients to identify expensive outliers.
- The "Cost Drivers" Heatmap: A correlation matrix showing which clinical events (e.g., ICU days vs. Ward days, Cardiac MRIs vs. standard Labs) have the highest correlation with massive bill spikes.
- Trajectory Plotting: A line chart showing the average cumulative cost day-by-day. We want to see exactly on which day (e.g., Day 3 or Day 4) the costs suddenly spike for patients who experience complications.
- Variance Analysis: Plot the initial Day 0 estimated cost versus the Final Real Cost to prove that static estimates are currently failing the hospital.

2. Machine Learning Modeling Strategy
Since Gautam Sir specifically requested predicting "kya hua day 1 pe for day 2 pe", we cannot use a simple static model. We will use Sequential/Time-Series approaches:
- Baseline Model (To prove a point): We will first train a basic Linear Regression using only Day 0 features (Age, Diagnosis). We will show that its predictions are terrible because it ignores the daily clinical timeline.
- The Real Model (Rolling XGBoost / LightGBM): We will use gradient boosting trees with "Rolling Features". 
  - Features at Day $t$: `cumulative_icu_days_so_far`, `procedures_done_today`, `total_meds_given`, `day_of_stay`.
  - Every day, the model ingests the new daily features and outputs an updated prediction for the Final Cost.
- Advanced Approach (Optional LSTM/RNN): If required, we can use a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network, which is the industry standard for time-series medical sequences.

3. Evaluation Metrics (How we prove it works to the PM)
- MAE (Mean Absolute Error) in INR: We will show the PM exactly how much money our prediction is off by (e.g., "Our model predicts the final bill with an average error of only ₹2,000").
- Day-Wise Convergence Curve: We will show a graph proving that as the patient's stay progresses (from Day 1 to Day 5), our model's prediction gets closer and closer to the actual final bill, eliminating surprise variances.
